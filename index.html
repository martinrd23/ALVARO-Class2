Present Perfect Simple

Experience / result

Use it when the exact finished time is not important, or when a past action has a present result.

I have taken several practice tests.
She has finished the report.

Unfinished time

Use it when the time period is still connected to now.

I have studied a lot this week.
We have had three classes this month.
Exam clue: If the sentence gives a finished past time such as yesterday, last year, in 2024, two days ago, use Simple Past — not Present Perfect.

just · already · yet

just

A very recent completed action.

I have just finished.

already

Something happened sooner than expected.

She has already submitted it.

yet

Common in questions and negatives, usually at the end.

Have you finished yet?
I haven't finished yet.

for · since

ExpressionUseExample
forDurationI have studied English for three years.
sinceStarting pointI have studied English since 2023.
Fast check: “How long?” → duration = for; starting point = since.

Present Perfect Continuous

Activity continuing until now

I have been studying for two hours.

The focus is on the activity and its duration.

Recent activity with a present effect

He is tired because he has been working all morning.

The activity may have just stopped or may still be continuing.

Simple vs Continuous: Simple often emphasizes a result or completed amount; Continuous often emphasizes duration or repeated activity.

Present Perfect vs Simple Past

MeaningPresent PerfectSimple Past
Life experience, no finished timeI have visited Cusco twice.—
Finished past time—I visited Cusco in 2024.
Unfinished time periodI have studied a lot this week.—
Finished time period—I studied a lot last week.
